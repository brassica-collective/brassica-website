Welcome to the Brassica Collective

We're building a multi-house collective spread across the northern suburbs of Naarm.

We're a project implementing our version of RAD Housing, which aims to:

Retrofit and Decommodify housing Collectively, and in Solidarity

We use housing as a lever for transformative justice: stewarding homes to practice personal and collective change so we can act in solidarity with broader movements for justice.

Through this work, we change ourselves.

more about our Values

Our project supports changing capacity and resources.

Each month we intentionally opt-in to collective governance and working crews. Even though the polycrisis feels urgent, we focus on having ways to contribute that work for different ways of thinking, being and doing.

how we Participate

What does multi-house collective mean to us?

Imagine having a room in a share-house in which you have the agency to shape your space, and the security that Australian rentals fail to provide.

But life comes with changes, and we live in changing times. We want to be able to switch houses to get closer to a school or a job, or perhaps because we're struggling in a relationship and want to live further from someone for a while.

To navigate this change, we imagine ending up as about fifty residents, spread across multiple share-houses, and perhaps some other kinds of dwellings with specialised features.

🌱 Seedling House

Our first site will be called Seedling House. Right now, we're developing the the plan for what to buy, and how we'll use it.

Our vision for Seedling house is that it will be A practice ground for community living ideally with at least five bedrooms, with capacity for participants and collaborates in the project to try out community by occasionally using the house for meals, working, events or staying the night.

Retrofit

We all need homes designed for the climate crisis. Designed for rapidly de-carbonised lifestyles, resilience in the face of extreme weather, and able to grow food.

Retrofitting existing homes is the fastest and most sustinable way to provide climate-ready housing, and Permaculturalists have already written up how to do this in temperate Naarm.

Decommodify

Our goal is to collectively buy around six suburban homes in inner northern Naarm suburbs, creating a multi-house cooperative of share-houses that gives us the agency to move around as our lives adapt to changing times.

We don't need to personally own these homes to achieve that, we can take them off the market, and steward them for both present and future residents. We leverage what capital and income we have between us. There's no shares, no buy-in, and no special rights for putting in money.

Collectively

Collectivising resources like housing is already an economic necessity for many, and will become even more so. This makes it vital to get good at the skills of working together and navigating conflicts big and small.

In the end, our project wont just house us, it'll help us change into the sort of people that we will need to be to in the coming decades. We can't afford to isolate ourselves from community, and we want a future that doesn't use oppression and punishment to resolve conflict.

Solidarity

If we sound like "doomers" about the climate crisis, perhaps we are. Doomers in the movies build bunkers hide from the world. We recognise that our liberation is bound up together, and we want our work to be an ongoing project engaged in active solidarity with others on the frontlines of the climate and housing crises.

Many of us in the Brassica Collective are also settlers on stolen Wurundjeri land. Any talk of stewarding housing for the future has to be firmly in the context of the ongoing struggle of all first-nations people for land back, justice and self-determination.
