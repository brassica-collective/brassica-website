## Attending in-person events with regard to contagious illness

It's important that participants can plan to attend in-person events with confidence that the risk that we will be exposed to contagious illness is being minimised by the actions we take collectively to care for each other.

While it sounds simple, this topic can be surprisingly complex and these guidelines are unlikely to cover all possible scenarios. We can change and add to these guidelines as we need to in order to keep building our collective understanding of what actions we can take and expect others to take to reduce the risk to each other of spreading contagious illnesses. 

### Key Terms

For these guidelines when we say **"contagious illness"** we are most often concerned with Covid 19 or influenza but the same guidelines apply to other illnesses that spread between people.

- **Influenza** is communicable for up to 24 hours prior to the onset of symptoms and up to 7 days after onset of symptoms in adults and longer in children (up to 10 days). The incubation period (how long it takes for a person to develop symptoms after exposure) is 1 to 4 days. It is transmitted by inhalation of respiratory droplets produced by an infected person while talking, coughing or sneezing and can also be spread through touching surfaces where infected droplets have landed. [source for this information](https\://www\.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/influenza).

- **Covid 19** is communicable for up to 48 hours prior to the onset of symptoms (or positive test if asymptomatic) until free of acute symptoms (or up to 10 days for people who are asymptomatic). The incubation period differs between strains, ranging from 1 to 14 days. It is transmitted from person to person through the inhalation of aerosolised particles or respiratory droplets. [source for this information](https\://www\.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/covid-19-coronavirus-disease-2019).

- "**Close contact**" means we spent more than 4hrs in an indoor setting (e.g. sharing a room or car) with a person during their infectious period. 

**Risk categories**: Brassica Collective has two risk categories for attending in-person events with regard to contagious illness:"stay away" risk category; and "ask and mask" risk category

- By **"stay away"** we mean: we communicate apologies and do not attend events in-person. We may request to be able to attend by hybrid format or for the event to be held online.

- By **"ask and mask"** we mean: at least 6hrs before the event we ask the people who plan to attend if they would feel comfortable attending alongside us under the circumstances (we may explain the circumstances to the best of our knowledge). If anyone who is planning to attend does not feel comfortable attending then we stay away (and everyone involved is encouraged to check in with our conduct supporters around how we feel about this situation). 

By **masking** we mean wearing a well-fitted medical mask (not a cloth mask, bandanna etc) at all times when in the presence of other attendees except to drink water, and using hand sanitiser regularly / before touching anything.

### Attendee Responsibilities & Expectations: 

As a starting point, we expect each other to:

- "stay away" when:  
  - we have symptoms of contagious illness (fever, cough, sore throat, sneezing, stuffy or runny nose etc).

- "ask and mask" when:  
  - at the tail end of an illness: 10 days after onset of symptoms, if we still have symptoms.  
  - if we are aware we could be infectious and asymptomatic:   
    - If we have been in **close contact** in the past 4 days with someone who we are aware was in an infectious period (who had symptoms or has since developed symptoms). 

### Trust and communication

Sometimes people have symptoms such as fatigue, coughing or runny nose from non-communicable conditions such as allergies. Let's think carefully about whether we could be sick and take precautions to confirm the symptoms are related to a non-communicable condition if possible. Consider communicating reassurance to other attendees if we are symptomatic with a non-communicable condition and attending a Brassica Collective event. 

## Review Conditions

These guidelines will be reviewed whenever a participant proposes an amendment to these guidelines

## Context Questions & Assumptions

Why are these guidelines not an agreement?

Rather than have every participant involved in every decision we delegate defined decision making scope (responsibility) to crews. The scope of responsibility that is delegated to a crew *is* a collective agreement and can be reviewed at any time by collective process. DAS crew is delegated the scope to “develop processes & guidelines for the handbook for areas that are voluntary and don’t need agreement (a somewhat grey area)”. This event attendance guidelines is in that grey area. It’s descriptive of our current practices. It's a voluntary guideline for participants attending events. If a participant thinks that this guideline needs to be changed they can join DAS crew and change it. If a participant thinks that this needs to be an agreement they can bring it as a proposal.
